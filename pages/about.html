About This Encyclopedia

Our Mission

The ESP / Arduino Pin Encyclopedia exists to make embedded hardware easier to understand.

Microcontrollers expose dozens of pins, alternate functions, buses, timers, ADC channels, interrupts and special hardware features. At the same time, the modules connected to those microcontrollers have their own electrical requirements, protocols and pinouts.

The goal of this encyclopedia is to bring that information together into one practical reference that can be used while designing, wiring, debugging and programming real hardware.

Source-of-Truth Philosophy

The encyclopedia is intended to be more than a collection of simplified pinout diagrams. Each hardware entry should eventually distinguish between the microcontroller itself, the development board and the individual breakout module.

Three different things must be distinguished:
  • MCU: the actual silicon and its electrical/peripheral capabilities.
  • Board: the development board exposing some subset of those MCU pins.
  • Module: the external hardware connected to the MCU.

This distinction is particularly important because a pin that exists on an MCU may not be exposed on a particular development board, and a breakout board may add level shifting, pull-up resistors, regulators or other circuitry.

How to Use the Encyclopedia

Recommended Workflow

  1. Identify the exact microcontroller or development board.
  2. Identify the exact peripheral or module being connected.
  3. Locate its pinout and electrical requirements.
  4. Determine which communication interface is being used.
  5. Check the MCU's available pins and alternate functions.
  6. Check voltage and logic-level compatibility.
  7. Check for pin conflicts with other peripherals.
  8. Wire the hardware.
  9. Configure the appropriate software library or peripheral.
  10. Test the hardware before building additional functionality.

Electrical Compatibility

Pin names alone do not guarantee that two devices can be connected directly.

CAUTION

Voltage

Confirm the supply voltage required by the module and the logic voltage used by its communication pins.

CAUTION

Current

Do not assume an MCU GPIO can safely power a peripheral. Check the actual current requirements and GPIO limits.

CAUTION

Pull-Ups

Interfaces such as I2C frequently depend on pull-up resistors. Verify whether the module already provides them.

CAUTION

Special Pins

Boot-strapping, flash-connected, input-only and other special-purpose pins may have restrictions.

Accuracy & Verification

Hardware documentation can contain significant differences between chips, development boards and breakout modules that appear superficially similar.

  • Prefer manufacturer datasheets and reference manuals for electrical specifications.
  • Distinguish MCU capabilities from development-board pin labels.
  • Identify board-specific circuitry such as regulators, level shifters and pull-up resistors.
  • Avoid assuming that similarly named modules are electrically identical.
  • Verify voltage and current requirements before connecting hardware.
  • Treat example wiring as a configuration rather than a universal pin assignment.
  • Preserve manufacturer-specific terminology where it matters for accurate hardware identification.

Design Philosophy

The encyclopedia is designed around a simple principle: make the hardware understandable before making the software complicated.

A good embedded project should make it clear what every wire does, what every pin supports, what voltage is present, which protocol is being used and why a particular pin was selected.

The goal is therefore not simply to provide pinout pictures. It is to build a reusable technical reference that connects the physical hardware layer to firmware, wiring and eventually simulation.

Reference at a Glance

MCU → Pin → Peripheral → Interface → Firmware

Every hardware entry in the encyclopedia should ultimately explain this entire chain.

Identify the hardware, understand its electrical requirements, select compatible pins, establish the communication interface, wire it correctly and then implement the firmware.
